TACIT: Tactile Contact Supervision for Spatial Attention in Dexterous Manipulation

TACIT uses touch recorded during demonstrations to teach a robot where to pay attention as it approaches an object.

Learning from contact

When the fingers touch the ball in a demonstration, their contact gives us a location. TACIT uses that location to teach attention on earlier camera frames. At inference, the policy predicts attention from the current camera points.

How the target and policy feature are computed

Active tactile taxels and calibrated fingertip poses locate the contact centroid in the camera cloud’s coordinate frame without an object pose estimate. The centroid defines a Gaussian target on sampled camera points. Frame selection checks the lead time to the chosen contact and whether the camera points support the target. A selected frame can follow an earlier contact.

The attention head scores raw points independently, primarily from base-frame XYZ for camera points. The policy receives a feature computed from the weighted camera points, peak relevance, and scaled entropy.

Real-robot results

Compared with the input-matched A2 baseline, TACIT raised task success from 10.0% to 66.7% for ball placement and from 20.0% to 73.3% for peg insertion at the evaluated checkpoints.

Robot setup and baselines

We collected ten demonstrations per task on a Franka FR3 with a LEAP hand and three 9DTact fingertips. A2 and TACIT each use 600 camera points and 900 tactile points. A1 is a vision-only reference with 1,500 camera points. The A1–A2 comparison changes both the input modality and the camera sampling budget.

TACIT task success exceeds A2 at all five ball placement regions and four of five peg regions. Placements are the independent unit. The table gives descriptive pooled counts for the evaluated checkpoints. See Paper §§4.3–4.4 and §5.1.

Scope and limitations

  • Workspace. Real tests use five demonstrated placement regions and one hardware setup. Held-out simulation covers new coordinates inside the training hull. Generalization outside the tested workspace remains untested.
  • What the attention shows. The head mainly scores camera points by position. The evidence supports spatial attention near the task object. Semantic object detection remains unverified.
  • What improved. TACIT mainly improves approach. Grasp stabilization, insertion, and release can still fail after the hand arrives.
